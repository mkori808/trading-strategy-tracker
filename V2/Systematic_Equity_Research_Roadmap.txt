======================================================================
1. PURPOSE
======================================================================

The objective is not to find one magical indicator that independently returns 20% per year.

The objective is to build a systematic equity portfolio from multiple validated,
independent sources of alpha that can ultimately produce approximately 20%+ long-run
net CAGR while materially outperforming SPY buy-and-hold on both absolute and
risk-adjusted performance.

This document should be used as the steering document for future research.


======================================================================
2. PRIMARY PERFORMANCE TARGETS
======================================================================

FINISHED-PORTFOLIO TARGET

Primary objective:
    Approximately 20% long-run NET CAGR

The portfolio should also:
    - Materially outperform SPY total-return buy-and-hold over comparable periods.
    - Produce statistically credible alpha rather than merely higher beta or factor exposure.
    - Maintain acceptable volatility and drawdowns.
    - Avoid dependence on extreme leverage.
    - Be implementable after realistic commissions, spreads, slippage, borrow costs,
      taxes where relevant, and turnover.
    - Remain robust across market regimes.
    - Have adequate capacity for the intended account size.
    - Behave in paper/live trading in a manner consistent with backtest assumptions.

INTERPRETATION OF LONG-RUN PORTFOLIO CAGR

    <10%        Generally unattractive unless risk is dramatically lower than SPY.
    10-15%      Potentially useful; complexity needs excellent risk-adjusted results.
    ~15%        Minimum compelling finished-portfolio outcome.
    ~20%        Primary target / excellent outcome.
    ~25%        Stretch target.
    30%+        Exceptional; do not make this a requirement.

The approximate 10% historical nominal S&P 500 total-return figure is a planning
baseline only. Historical experiments must compare against SPY's ACTUAL total-return
performance over the same dates.

A strategy returning 20% during a period when SPY also returns 20% has not achieved
the project's objective.

Likewise, a strategy returning 15% with much lower volatility, drawdown, and beta
may be more valuable than a 20% strategy that takes dramatically more risk.


======================================================================
3. INDIVIDUAL EDGE VS. FINISHED PORTFOLIO
======================================================================

Do NOT require every individual strategy or signal to produce 15-20% CAGR.

An individual edge can be extremely valuable if it has characteristics such as:
    - 3-8% genuine annual alpha.
    - Strong statistical evidence.
    - Low correlation with existing strategies.
    - Low or manageable correlation with SPY.
    - Robustness across regimes and subperiods.
    - Realistic implementation costs.
    - Sufficient capacity.
    - A plausible economic or behavioral mechanism.

Example:

    A strategy with:
        4-6% annual alpha
        Sharpe around 1.2-1.5
        low correlation to the existing alpha engine
        stable regime behavior
        realistic costs

    can be an outstanding discovery even if its standalone CAGR is nowhere near 20%.

The research goal is therefore:

    FIND REPEATABLE, STATISTICALLY SUPPORTED SOURCES OF ALPHA.

The portfolio goal is:

    COMBINE INDEPENDENT EDGES TO MAXIMIZE NET RISK-ADJUSTED EXCESS RETURN.

The stretch performance goal is:

    BUILD THE COMBINED PORTFOLIO TOWARD >=20% LONG-RUN NET CAGR.

them. This is important both statistically and for debugging.

======================================================================

The strongest emerging economic hypothesis is not simply:

    "Stocks mean revert."

It is closer to:

    TEMPORARY LIQUIDITY / TECHNICAL PRICE PRESSURE -> REVERSAL

    GENUINE NEW INFORMATION -> CONTINUATION OR NO REVERSAL

Evidence pointing in this direction includes:

    - Extremely strong non-earnings IBS.
    - Earnings-day IBS losing or reversing its effect.
    - Strong RSI2 / short-term reversal evidence.
    - Evidence that some extreme losers continue falling rather than bouncing.
    - Gap/event-type behavior that may differ from ordinary technical dislocations.

This may be the most important clue produced by the research platform so far.

The next stage should try to distinguish:
    "temporary price pressure"
from
    "information-driven repricing."

10. NEXT RESEARCH PRIORITIES
======================================================================

PRIORITY 1 — DEEPEN THE IBS / REVERSAL MECHANISM

Core hypothesis:

    Short-term mean reversion should be strongest when a move looks liquidity-driven
    or technically driven and weakest, absent, or reversed when the move appears
    information-driven.

Test conditioning variables ONE AT A TIME before building another composite.

Candidate conditioning variables:
    - earnings-event flag
    - overnight gap as fraction of total move
    - abnormal volume
    - turnover
    - liquidity
    - intraday range
    - idiosyncratic volatility
    - market capitalization
    - liquidity bucket
    - volatility bucket
    - market regime

Goal:
    discover WHEN IBS/reversal works, not another oscillator that restates IBS.

Avoid:
    adding many RSI/oscillator/EMA-distance variants that are likely to be redundant.


PRIORITY 2 — TEST WHETHER IBS SHOULD TRADE MORE FREQUENTLY

Current mismatch:

    strongest IBS evidence = approximately 1-day horizon
    current implementation = weekly rebalance

Do not alter the existing paper tracks.

Instead create a separate prospective execution experiment.

Candidate variants:
    - daily IBS
    - every-two-day IBS
    - weekly IBS
    - daily with rank/no-trade buffers
    - turnover-constrained daily IBS

Evaluate:
    - net alpha
    - turnover
    - spread/slippage
    - alpha captured per unit turnover
    - drawdown
    - implementation burden

The goal is not maximum gross alpha.
The goal is maximum IMPLEMENTABLE net value.


PRIORITY 3 — BUILD AN ORTHOGONAL EVENT / INFORMATION-CONTINUATION ENGINE

The existing portfolio is heavily dominated by mean reversion.

A high-priority new family should have the opposite economic mechanism:
    continuation after genuine information.

Earnings events are the obvious starting point because the IBS conditioning result
already indicates that earnings days behave differently.

Possible daily-data hypotheses:
    - earnings-gap direction
    - earnings open-to-close continuation
    - post-earnings 1-day continuation
    - post-earnings 5-day continuation
    - post-earnings 10-day continuation
    - abnormal-volume event continuation

This should be developed as its own preregistered signal family rather than being
quietly folded into IBS.


PRIORITY 4 — MONETIZE THE NEGATIVE / SHORT SIDE

Several tested variables appear much better at identifying LOSERS than WINNERS.

Potential sources include:
    - weak fundamental / quality tails
    - high net share issuance
    - extreme information-driven losers
    - other signals whose long leg is weak but whose bottom-ranked stocks underperform

A long-only architecture discards this information.

Create a separate prospective short research program.

Requirements:
    - focus first on liquid/easy-to-borrow securities
    - record actual broker borrowability prospectively
    - record actual borrow fees
    - include short-specific execution costs
    - evaluate short legs independently before combining them
    - do not assume historical borrow was available merely because a stock existed

Potential endpoint:
    a long/short or market-neutral alpha sleeve that can diversify the long-only engine.


PRIORITY 5 — EXPLOIT SMALL-CAP / CAPACITY ADVANTAGES CAREFULLY

Small capital can potentially exploit opportunities that are unattractive to large
institutions because of:
    - limited capacity
    - lower liquidity
    - high operational overhead relative to dollars deployed
    - higher turnover
    - niche event-driven opportunities

But V1 demonstrated the danger:
    historical small-cap alpha can be highly regime-dependent.

Therefore capacity should be tested directly.

Measure signal strength across:
    - market-cap buckets
    - dollar-volume buckets
    - spread/liquidity buckets

Compare:
    gross edge
    vs.
    realistic trading cost and capacity

Do not automatically equate "small cap" with "retail edge."


PRIORITY 6 — NONLINEAR CONDITIONAL MODELING

Machine learning should come AFTER individual mechanisms are understood.

The purpose of ML here is not:
    "feed the system hundreds of technical indicators and let it find alpha."

The purpose is:
    model nonlinear interactions among already-vetted predictors.

Potential features:
    - IBS
    - RSI2
    - Sector RS
    - short-term reversal
    - size
    - liquidity / turnover
    - volatility
    - overnight gap
    - earnings/event flag
    - validated fundamental-negative variables

Candidate modeling approach:
    shallow / constrained boosted trees or another interpretable nonlinear model.

Primary question:
    Can conditional interactions improve out-of-sample IC / alpha beyond a simple
    hand-weighted composite?

Maintain strict multiple-testing controls.

11. WHAT NOT TO DO NEXT
======================================================================

Do NOT:

    - Search randomly through hundreds of indicators.
    - Optimize directly for maximum CAGR.
    - Add another oscillator simply because IBS and RSI2 worked.
    - Reuse spent holdout data as if it were untouched.
    - Reject a 3-8% genuine alpha source because it does not independently make 20%.
    - Combine unvalidated signals before understanding them individually.
    - Assume negative long performance means a signal is useless.
    - Assume small caps automatically provide exploitable alpha.
    - Ignore turnover, spreads, slippage, borrow fees, or capacity.
    - Modify current paper tracks in reaction to interim performance.
    - Treat six months of paper returns as enough time to statistically prove a
      modest annual alpha.

12. HOW TO EVALUATE THE PAPER PERIOD
======================================================================

The 26-week paper period is primarily an IMPLEMENTATION and CONSISTENCY test.

Do not expect six months to prove a modest annual alpha with a t-statistic above 2.

At the paper checkpoint, evaluate:

    - Did the system run correctly?
    - Did signal calculations match the research implementation?
    - Were virtual fills realistic?
    - Was realized slippage consistent with assumptions?
    - Did turnover match expectations?
    - Did cross-sectional IC generally have the expected sign?
    - Did V3.1 rank stocks better than V2?
    - Did V3.1 add value beyond IBS alone?
    - Did earnings-conditioned IBS behave as expected?
    - Were drawdowns consistent with historical expectations?
    - Were factor exposures stable?
    - Were any operational assumptions unrealistic?
    - Were there unexplained divergences between modeled and observed execution?

Do NOT rewrite the strategy because of a few bad weeks.

13. PORTFOLIO ARCHITECTURE TO AIM TOWARD
======================================================================

Do not assume the final system will be one stock-ranking model.

A plausible eventual architecture is:

    CORE MARKET / BETA EXPOSURE
        +
    ALPHA ENGINE

The alpha engine may contain multiple sleeves:

    Sleeve A:
        short-horizon liquidity / mean reversion
        (IBS / RSI2 family)

    Sleeve B:
        event / information continuation

    Sleeve C:
        slower sector / relative-strength effects

    Sleeve D:
        negative-selection / short / market-neutral anomalies

    Sleeve E:
        future orthogonal discoveries

The purpose of diversification is not simply to add expected returns together.

If independent sleeves increase the combined portfolio's Sharpe ratio, the portfolio
may support more active risk while maintaining tolerable drawdowns.

That is how several modest but genuine edges can become a high-return finished system.

15. DECISION FRAMEWORK FOR NEW SIGNALS
======================================================================

For each new hypothesis, ask:

1. Is there a plausible economic or behavioral mechanism?

2. Is it genuinely different from what is already in the system?

3. Does it predict returns individually before being combined?

4. Does the effect survive realistic execution timing?

5. Does it survive costs?

6. Is the effect concentrated in one historical regime?

7. Does it survive across size and liquidity groups?

8. Is the long leg useful, the short leg useful, or both?

9. What horizon contains the signal?

10. Does it improve the portfolio after considering correlation with existing edges?

Classify the result:

    SUPPORTED
        Evidence is strong enough to continue.

    FALSIFIED
        The preregistered economic claim is contradicted by adequate evidence.

    UNRESOLVED / UNDERPOWERED
        Evidence is insufficient to confidently support or reject the hypothesis.

Avoid calling an underpowered test a failure merely because p > 0.05.

17. THE CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTION
======================================================================

The most promising current question is:

    CAN WE DISTINGUISH TEMPORARY PRICE PRESSURE FROM GENUINE INFORMATION-DRIVEN MOVES?

If yes:

    temporary dislocation
        -> mean-reversion engine

    genuine information shock
        -> continuation/event engine

This would create two economically distinct sides of the same market process and
could be substantially more powerful than simply adding more technical indicators.

18. PROJECT NORTH STAR
======================================================================

The project is NOT trying to prove that one signal can make 20% per year.

The project is trying to build an evidence-based collection of independent alpha
engines and combine them intelligently.

FINAL NORTH STAR:

    Build a systematic equity portfolio capable of >=20% long-run net CAGR while
    materially outperforming SPY total-return buy-and-hold on both absolute and
    risk-adjusted performance, with the excess return supported by statistically
    credible alpha rather than merely greater beta, factor exposure, or leverage.

TARGET INTERPRETATION:

    ~15% CAGR
        Lower bound where the finished product starts becoming genuinely compelling.

    ~20% CAGR
        Primary target.

    ~25% CAGR
        Stretch target / exceptional outcome.

    ~30%+ CAGR
        Outstanding and unusual; require correspondingly stronger evidence.

INDIVIDUAL EDGE STANDARD:

    Do not judge individual discoveries by the 20% portfolio target.

    A robust 3-8% source of annual alpha with strong statistics, manageable costs,
    and low correlation to existing strategies may be exactly what the portfolio needs.

RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY:

    Find real edges.
    Understand their mechanisms.
    Validate them independently.
    Preserve forward evidence.
    Combine orthogonal edges.
    Control implementation costs.
    Scale only after the alpha is proven.

That is the path toward the 20%+ portfolio.
